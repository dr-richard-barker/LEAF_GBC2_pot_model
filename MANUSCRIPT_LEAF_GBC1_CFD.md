## Abstract

Plant growth habitats deployed on lunar surface landers and orbital platforms operate in fractional gravity ($0.166\,g$ on the Moon) or microgravity ($\mu g$), where buoyancy-driven natural thermal and solutal convection is suppressed by $83.4\%$ to $100\%$. Without compensatory forced ventilation, stagnant boundary layers envelop foliar surfaces, throttling carbon dioxide ($\text{CO}_2$) assimilation, trapping transpired water vapor ($\text{H}_2\text{O}$), elevating leaf temperature, and inducing nocturnal respiratory hypercapnia. Here, we present a three-dimensional Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD), porous-media multiphasic transport, and biochemical gas-exchange framework built directly from the flight-like Computer-Aided Design (CAD) geometry of the **Lunar Effects on Agricultural Flora (LEAF) Growth Chamber 1 (GBC1)** assembly (`M09024F-MDL-5.STEP` and `M09025F-MDL-2.STEP`). We model the exact three-compartment interior featuring a **3-part partitioned porous rooting substrate** ($Y = 7.62\text{--}14.62\ \text{mm}$, total void volume $5.28\ \text{mL}$ at porosity $\epsilon = 0.50$) and asymmetric multi-species planting densities: **32 *Brassica rapa* seeds** in each lateral compartment (Chambers 1 and 3; tall erect canopy reaching $Y = 26.0\ \text{mm}$, total foliar area $64.0\ \text{cm}^2/\text{chamber}$, $\text{LAI} = 9.44$) flanking **100 *Arabidopsis thaliana* seeds** in the central compartment beneath the exhaust port (Chamber 2; miniature prostrate rosette lawn reaching $Y = 19.5\ \text{mm}$, total foliar area $15.0\ \text{cm}^2$, $\text{LAI} = 3.09$).

Across 21 parametric flow sweeps ($Q = 0.05\text{--}4.0\ \text{L/min}$), 3D Mean Age-of-Air ($\theta$) partial differential equation (PDE) inversions, 14-day developmental growth time-series, and off-nominal spacecraft fault evaluations, we establish four core engineering findings:
1. **Aerodynamic "Dip-and-Converge" Channelling & Uniformity:** The stature differential between the tall *B. rapa* canopies ($\Delta Y = 11.4\ \text{mm}$) and the low-lying *A. thaliana* rosette lawn ($\Delta Y = 4.9\ \text{mm}$) creates a $14.2\ \text{mm}$ open central plenum under Port 2 ($Z = 0\ \text{mm}$). Forced air entering Port 1 ($Z = -21.59\ \text{mm}$) skims across Chamber 1, dips into the low-resistance central Chamber 2 plenum, and converges with return flow from Chamber 3, achieving $<1.0\%$ inter-chamber Mean Age-of-Air disparity ($\theta = 6.05\text{--}6.10\ \text{s}$ at $Q = 0.50\ \text{L/min}$; Sandberg conservation ratio $\theta_{\text{out}}/\tau = 0.938$).
2. **Lunar Buoyancy Suppression & Daytime Compensatory Flow:** At the nominal Earth pump rate ($Q = 0.50\ \text{L/min}$), Lunar gravity ($1.62\ \text{m/s}^2$) reduces leaf boundary-layer conductance ($g_{bl}$) by $36.3\%$ (from $0.0604$ to $0.0385\ \text{mol m}^{-2}\ \text{s}^{-1}$ in *B. rapa* and $0.0759$ to $0.0484\ \text{mol m}^{-2}\ \text{s}^{-1}$ in *A. thaliana*), depressing Farquhar–von Caemmerer–Berry (FvCB) net photosynthesis ($A_{\text{net}}$) to $81.5\%$ ($10.63\ \mu\text{mol m}^{-2}\ \text{s}^{-1}$) and $83.8\%$ ($8.43\ \mu\text{mol m}^{-2}\ \text{s}^{-1}$) of Earth $1g$ baselines while elevating leaf temperatures by $+2.42\ ^\circ\text{C}$. Increasing daytime flow to **$Q = 0.85\ \text{L/min}$** reduces canopy $\text{CO}_2$ drawdown and humidity accumulation by $37\%$, restoring $98\%$ of terrestrial microclimate scalar uniformity.
3. **Diurnal Photoperiod Inversion & Nocturnal Pump Setback Floor:** During the 8-hour dark skotoperiod, radiative leaf cooling ($\Delta T = -0.3\ \text{K}$) and dense mitochondrial $\text{CO}_2$ emission ($\beta_{\text{CO}_2} = -0.517$) invert buoyancy forces ($\text{Ri} < 0$), causing respiratory $\text{CO}_2$ to pool inside the lower drainage wells ($Y < 7.62\ \text{mm}$) and porous substrate. Under Lunar $0.166\,g$, low-power sleep setbacks ($Q \le 0.10\ \text{L/min}$) trigger severe canopy hypercapnia; maintaining a **nocturnal flow floor of $Q_{\text{night}} \ge 0.60\ \text{L/min}$** (or a $50\%$ setback to $0.425\ \text{L/min}$ from a $0.85\ \text{L/min}$ day baseline) prevents respiratory pooling while conserving transpired moisture ($+0.88\ \text{mmol m}^{-3}$).
4. **Bottom-Up RNAlater Perfusion & Fault Resilience:** Injecting saturated ammonium sulfate fixative (RNAlater; $\rho = 1250\ \text{kg/m}^3$, $\mu = 1.85\ \text{mPa}\cdot\text{s}$) at $Q_{\text{inj}} = 10\ \text{mL/min}$ through the Port 3 dipping nozzle ($Y = 9.5\ \text{mm}$ inside Substrate Block 3) saturates the 3-part root matrix in **$31.7\ \text{s}$** and submerges the entire $64.6\ \text{cm}^3$ chamber in **$6.46\ \text{min}$** at a peak pressure drop of **$0.73\ \text{kPa}$** ($4.04\ \text{kPa}$ after 14 days of root-driven porosity compaction to $\epsilon = 0.34$), remaining safely below the $5.0\ \text{kPa}$ peristaltic seal safety limit.

## 1. Introduction

Spaceflight plant biology experiments conducted aboard the International Space Station (ISS)—including NASA's Biological Research in Canisters (BRIC), Petri-dish fixation hardware (CARA), and the Vegetable Production System (Veggie)—have demonstrated that reduced gravity alters plant transcriptomes, cell wall remodeling, phytohormone signaling (auxin, abscisic acid, and ethylene), and photosynthetic carbon allocation. A major physical driver of these spaceflight phenotypes is not direct gravitropism alone, but the secondary **fluid-dynamic microclimate artifact** caused by the loss of buoyancy-driven natural convection:

$$\text{Gr}_L = \frac{g \, \beta_{\text{eff}} \, \Delta \Phi \, L_{\text{leaf}}^3}{\nu^2}$$

On Earth ($g = 9.81\ \text{m/s}^2$), warm, humidified air parcels adjacent to illuminated leaves rise rapidly via thermal ($\beta_T = 3.39 \times 10^{-3}\ \text{K}^{-1}$) and solutal water-vapor ($\beta_{\text{H}_2\text{O}} = +0.379$) buoyancy, continuously stripping away the diffusive boundary layer even when mechanical fan velocities are low. On the lunar surface ($g = 1.62\ \text{m/s}^2$, $0.166\,g$), buoyant acceleration drops by $83.4\%$, thickening the laminar sublayer $\delta_{bl}$ and shifting the mixed-convection Richardson number ($\text{Ri} = \text{Gr}/\text{Re}^2$) toward diffusion-limited regimes unless forced ventilation is specifically engineered to compensate.

The **LEAF Growth Chamber 1 (GBC1)** hardware addresses these challenges through an actively ventilated, hermetically sealed polycarbonate growth pot (`M09024F-MDL-5`) mated with a three-port fluidic distribution lid (`M09025F-MDL-2`). Unlike single-species chambers, the GBC1 mission architecture co-cultivates two model Brassicaceae species with starkly contrasting growth habits inside three baffled compartments sharing a common overhead air plenum:
- **Chambers 1 and 3 (Lateral Compartments):** Each seeded with **32 seeds of *Brassica rapa*** (fast-growing, broad-leaved, upright stems reaching $Y = 26.0\ \text{mm}$).
- **Chamber 2 (Central Compartment):** Seeded with **100 seeds of *Arabidopsis thaliana*** (miniature, high-density prostrate rosette lawn remaining below $Y = 19.5\ \text{mm}$).

Furthermore, end-of-mission transcriptomic preservation requires in-situ liquid fixation via **RNAlater** injected into the root zone to displace headspace gas through the central exhaust port without causing premature foliar splashing, gas pocket trapping, or mechanical stem shearing.

In this work, we construct a high-resolution 3D voxelized CFD and multiphasic transport digital twin directly from the STEP CAD assemblies of the LEAF GBC1 chamber. We quantify the coupled aerodynamics, residence-time distributions, species-specific carbon/water fluxes, diurnal photoperiod inversions, 14-day developmental ontogeny, and off-nominal spacecraft fault envelopes required to certify the GBC1 flight software and pump operating schedule.

### 2.3 Governing Fluid, Transport, and Biochemical Equations

#### 2.3.1 Incompressible Navier–Stokes with Boussinesq Thermal & Solutal Buoyancy
Airflow within the headspace, porous substrate, and plant canopies ([`sim/gbc1_cfd_engine.py`](file:///Users/drb_laptop/Documents/LEAF_GBC1_pot_model/sim/gbc1_cfd_engine.py)) is governed by continuity and the Brinkman-penalized momentum equations:

$$\nabla \cdot \mathbf{u} = 0$$

$$\frac{\partial \mathbf{u}}{\partial t} + (\mathbf{u} \cdot \nabla)\mathbf{u} = -\frac{1}{\rho_0}\nabla p + \nu \nabla^2 \mathbf{u} - \mathcal{D}(\mathbf{x})\mathbf{u} + \mathbf{g} \left[ \beta_T (T - T_0) + \beta_{\text{H}_2\text{O}}(C_{\text{H}_2\text{O}} - C_{\text{H}_2\text{O},0}) + \beta_{\text{CO}_2}(C_{\text{CO}_2} - C_{\text{CO}_2,0}) \right]$$

where $\rho_0 = 1.204\ \text{kg/m}^3$, $\nu = 1.51 \times 10^{-5}\ \text{m}^2/\text{s}$ at $22\ ^\circ\text{C}$ and $1\ \text{atm}$, and the spatially varying momentum sink $\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{x})$ equals $\frac{\nu}{\epsilon K}$ inside the three porous substrate blocks ($Y \in [7.62, 14.62]\ \text{mm}$) and $C_d \, a_{\text{leaf}}(\mathbf{x}) \, U_{\text{eff}}$ inside the differentiated plant canopies. Gravity $\mathbf{g} = [0, -g, 0]^T$ is evaluated across $g = 9.81\ \text{m/s}^2$ (Earth $1.0\,g$), $g = 1.62\ \text{m/s}^2$ (Lunar $0.166\,g$), and $g = 0.0\ \text{m/s}^2$ ($\mu g$).

#### 2.3.2 Multi-Species Advection–Diffusion & Mean Age of Air ($\theta$)
Dissolved/gaseous scalar deviations for $\text{CO}_2$ ($D_{\text{CO}_2} = 1.60 \times 10^{-5}\ \text{m}^2/\text{s}$) and water vapor ($D_{\text{H}_2\text{O}} = 2.42 \times 10^{-5}\ \text{m}^2/\text{s}$) satisfy:

$$\frac{\partial C_k}{\partial t} + \mathbf{u} \cdot \nabla C_k = D_k \nabla^2 C_k + S_k(\mathbf{x})$$

where volumetric sink/source rates $S_{\text{CO}_2}$ and $S_{\text{H}_2\text{O}}$ scale with total chamber foliar area ($-40.0\ \mu\text{mol m}^{-3}\ \text{s}^{-1}$ and $+80.0\ \text{mmol m}^{-3}\ \text{s}^{-1}$ in Chambers 1 & 3 vs. $-15.0\ \mu\text{mol m}^{-3}\ \text{s}^{-1}$ and $+30.0\ \text{mmol m}^{-3}\ \text{s}^{-1}$ in Chamber 2 during daytime illumination).

To rigorously verify chamber ventilation efficiency and detect dead zones ([`sim/advanced_analytics.py`](file:///Users/drb_laptop/Documents/LEAF_GBC1_pot_model/sim/advanced_analytics.py)), we solve the steady-state **Mean Age of Air** transport equation:

$$\mathbf{u} \cdot \nabla \theta - D_\theta \nabla^2 \theta = 1, \qquad \theta\big|_{\text{Port 1}} = 0, \qquad \frac{\partial \theta}{\partial n}\Bigg|_{\text{walls}} = 0$$

and validate global mass conservation against the **Sandberg Residence-Time Identity**:

$$\langle \theta \rangle_{\text{outlet, flux}} = \frac{\int_{\text{Port 2}} (\mathbf{u} \cdot \mathbf{n}) \, \theta \, dA}{\int_{\text{Port 2}} (\mathbf{u} \cdot \mathbf{n}) \, dA} \equiv \tau_{\text{nominal}} = \frac{V_{\text{fluid}}}{Q}$$

#### 2.3.3 Mixed-Convection Boundary-Layer Conductance & FvCB Photosynthesis
Within each compartment $c \in \{1, 2, 3\}$, the effective laminar leaf boundary-layer thickness $\delta_{\text{eff}, c}$ blends forced Blasius flat-plate convection ($\delta_{\text{forced}, c} = 4.92 \sqrt{\nu L_{\text{leaf}, c} / |\mathbf{u}_c|}$) and natural buoyant Grashof convection ($\delta_{\text{nat}, c} = 3.5 L_{\text{leaf}, c} \text{Gr}_c^{-1/4}$) via Churchill's asymptotic cube-root law:

$$\delta_{\text{eff}, c} = \left( \delta_{\text{forced}, c}^{-3} + \delta_{\text{nat}, c}^{-3} \right)^{-1/3}, \qquad g_{bl, c} = \frac{D_{\text{CO}_2}}{\delta_{\text{eff}, c} \, V_{\text{mol}}}$$

Net carbon assimilation $A_{\text{net}}$ ($\mu\text{mol CO}_2\ \text{m}^{-2}\ \text{s}^{-1}$) is obtained by analytically solving the coupled quadratic **Farquhar–von Caemmerer–Berry (FvCB)** biochemical demand and boundary-plus-stomatal supply equations ($g_{\text{tot}}^{-1} = g_{bl}^{-1} + g_s^{-1}$):

$$A_{\text{net}} = \min(A_c, A_j, A_p) - R_d, \qquad A = g_{\text{tot}}(C_s - C_i)$$

where $A_c$ is the Rubisco carboxylation-limited rate, $A_j$ is the RuBP electron-transport-limited rate, $A_p$ is the triose-phosphate utilization (TPU) ceiling at super-elevated cabin $\text{CO}_2$, and $C_s = C_{\text{amb}} - \Delta[\text{CO}_2]_{\text{canopy}}$.

#### 2.3.4 Two-Stage Harvest RNAlater Perfusion & Kozeny–Carman Root Compaction
During end-of-mission harvest ([`sim/simulate_rnalater_perfusion.py`](file:///Users/drb_laptop/Documents/LEAF_GBC1_pot_model/sim/simulate_rnalater_perfusion.py)), aqueous RNAlater ($\rho_{\text{RNA}} = 1250\ \text{kg/m}^3$, $\mu_{\text{RNA}} = 1.85 \times 10^{-3}\ \text{Pa}\cdot\text{s}$, surface tension $\sigma = 0.078\ \text{N/m}$) is pumped into Port 3 and discharged at $Y = 9.5\ \text{mm}$ inside Substrate Block 3. Stage 1 models radial and inter-chamber Darcy imbibition across the three substrate blocks ($V_{\text{void}} = 5.28\ \text{mL}$), while Stage 2 tracks upward Volume-of-Fluid (VOF) free-surface displacement through the $54.05\ \text{mL}$ headspace toward the Port 2 exhaust vent. Root volume expansion over the 14-day mission compresses substrate porosity from $\epsilon_0 = 0.50$ to $\epsilon(t)$, reducing permeability according to the **Kozeny–Carman relation**:

$$K(\epsilon) = K_0 \frac{\epsilon^3 (1 - \epsilon_0)^2}{\epsilon_0^3 (1 - \epsilon)^2}$$

#### 2.3.5 Porous Substrate Capillarity (van Genuchten / Richards) & Optical Lid Condensation Defense
To evaluate root hydration uniformity and oxygen availability in partial gravity ($0.166\,g$), unsaturated capillary retention within the $7.0\ \text{mm}$ calcined clay ceramic substrate blocks ($Y \in [7.62, 14.62]\ \text{mm}$, total porosity $\phi = 0.58\ \text{m}^3/\text{m}^3$, residual water $\theta_r = 0.08$, saturated water $\theta_s = 0.55$) is solved using the **Richards equation** and the **van Genuchten retention model** ([`sim/simulate_water_capillarity_and_humidity.py`](file:///Users/drb_laptop/Documents/LEAF_GBC1_pot_model/sim/simulate_water_capillarity_and_humidity.py)):

$$\frac{\partial \theta_w}{\partial t} = \nabla \cdot \left[ K(\psi) \nabla (\psi + z \cdot g/g_0) \right] - S_{\text{root}}(x, y, z)$$

$$\Theta(\psi) = \frac{\theta_w - \theta_r}{\theta_s - \theta_r} = \left[ 1 + (\alpha |\psi|)^n \right]^{-m}, \qquad K(\Theta) = K_s \Theta^{1/2} \left[ 1 - (1 - \Theta^{1/m})^m \right]^2$$

where $\alpha = 0.082\ \text{cm}^{-1}$ and $n = 1.95$ ($m = 1 - 1/n = 0.487$). In Lunar gravity ($g = 1.62\ \text{m/s}^2$), gravity drainage is attenuated by $83.4\%$, so capillary suction ($P_c = 2\sigma \cos\theta_c / r_{\text{pore}}$) dominates, yielding an exceptionally flat vertical moisture profile. Rhizosphere aeration is monitored via air-filled porosity $\epsilon_{\text{air}}(y) = \phi - \theta_w(y)$ and Millington–Quirk effective oxygen diffusivity:

$$D_{\text{eff, O}_2}(y) = D_{0, \text{O}_2} \frac{\epsilon_{\text{air}}(y)^{10/3}}{\phi^2}$$

Whole-chamber water vapor conservation across the 164 transpiring plants (64 *B. rapa* + 100 *A. thaliana*) couples Penman–Monteith foliar transpiration $E_{\text{canopy}} = g_{tw} \cdot \frac{e_{\text{sat}}(T_{\text{leaf}}) - e_{\text{chamber}}}{P_{\text{atm}}}$ to the ventilation scavenging rate $Q$. The inner optical surface temperature of the $3.0\ \text{mm}$ acrylic lid ($T_{\text{lid}}$) is compared to the chamber dew point ($T_{\text{dew}}$) to compute the **optical condensation safety margin**:

$$\Delta T_{\text{margin}}(Q) = T_{\text{lid}}(Q) - T_{\text{dew}}(Q)$$

where $\Delta T_{\text{margin}} > 0\ ^\circ\text{C}$ guarantees a clear, droplet-free imaging window for growth tracking cameras.

### 3.9 Model Lunar LEAF: 5-Day Spaceflight Simulation, Nytex Root Barrier Mechanics, and Push vs. Pull Aerodynamic Regimes (Figures 12–16)

   At mission initiation ($t = 0\ \text{h}$), DI water is injected through Port 3 into the porous foam cassettes ($\phi = 0.70$, $V_{\text{bulk}} = 10.55\ \text{cm}^3$). Full saturation ($S_w = 1.0$) is achieved at an injection volume of **$V_{\text{act}} = 7.39\ \text{mL}$** (or $5.28\ \text{mL}$ for $\phi = 0.50$), establishing uniform capillary moisture transport ($\theta_w = 0.70$) without free liquid detachment under lunar $0.166g$. By the Young–Laplace equation:
   $$P_{\text{bubble}} = \frac{4\sigma \cos\theta_c}{d_{\text{pore}}} = \frac{4(0.0728\ \text{N/m})\cos(15^\circ)}{35 \times 10^{-6}\ \text{m}} = 8.04\ \text{kPa}$$

   - *Push Mode (Port 1 Inflow):* Generates positive chamber gauge pressure ($\Delta P_{\text{gauge}} = +1.2\text{ to }+14.5\ \text{Pa}$ across $Q = 0.2\text{--}1.2\ \text{L/min}$). While effective for boundary layer scrubbing, positive internal pressure creates an outward leakage hazard into the spacecraft crew cabin.
   - *Pull Mode (Port 2 Suction):* Generates negative chamber gauge pressure ($\Delta P_{\text{gauge}} = -1.0\text{ to }-11.8\ \text{Pa}$), establishing intrinsic aerodynamic **bio-containment**. Headspace air is smoothly drawn from Port 1 and perimeter seams, converging symmetrically into the central Chamber 2 open plenum above the low-profile *Arabidopsis* lawn ($\gamma > 0.95$). Pull mode is recommended as the primary flight operating mode for crewed missions.

### 3.11 Comparative Spaceflight Hardware Benchmark: Grounding in Empirical Gas-Exchange Fluxes (Figure 18)

To position the LEAF GBC1 architecture within space biology flight heritage, we synthesized a multi-system benchmark comparing GBC1 against NASA BRIC, CARA, and VEGGIE hardware ([`sim/generate_spaceflight_hardware_comparison.py`](file:///Users/drb_laptop/Documents/LEAF_GBC1_pot_model/sim/generate_spaceflight_hardware_comparison.py), [`figures/fig18_spaceflight_hardware_comprehensive_benchmark.png`](file:///Users/drb_laptop/Documents/LEAF_GBC1_pot_model/figures/fig18_spaceflight_hardware_comprehensive_benchmark.png), [`results/tables/gbc1_spaceflight_hardware_comprehensive_benchmark.csv`](file:///Users/drb_laptop/Documents/LEAF_GBC1_pot_model/results/tables/gbc1_spaceflight_hardware_comprehensive_benchmark.csv)):
1. **Empirical Gas-Exchange Anchoring:**
   Foliar source terms were anchored to whole-chamber Vernier empirical gas-exchange measurements on *Arabidopsis thaliana* (Col-0) ($A_{\text{net}} = 3.85\ \mu\text{mol m}^{-2}\ \text{s}^{-1}$, dark respiration $R_{\text{dark}} = 1.18\ \mu\text{mol m}^{-2}\ \text{s}^{-1}$, $R/A = 0.31$, transpiration near saturation $E_{\text{sat}} = 0.005\ \text{mmol H}_2\text{O m}^{-2}\ \text{s}^{-1}$) and *Brassica rapa* ($A_{\text{net}} = 10.27\ \mu\text{mol m}^{-2}\ \text{s}^{-1}$).
2. **Carbon Starvation Kinetics ($\tau_{\text{starve}}$):**
   - *NASA BRIC (60 mm hermetically sealed Petri dish, $10.8\ \text{cm}^3$ headspace):* At a basal seedling assimilation rate of $3.85\ \mu\text{mol m}^{-2}\ \text{s}^{-1}$, the sealed internal carbon pool ($400\ \text{ppm}\ \text{CO}_2$) is exhausted in **$\tau_{\text{starve}} = 7.0\ \text{minutes}$**, after which photosynthesis completely self-limits into severe starvation ($\Delta C = -125\ \text{ppm}$). In the dark, respiration consumes oxygen to $5\%$ hypoxia within $6.5\ \text{days}$.
   - *NASA CARA (120 mm microporous taped dish, $68.2\ \text{cm}^3$ headspace):* Perimeter tape diffusion yields an air exchange rate of only $\text{ACH} = 1.76\ \text{h}^{-1}$ (one turnover every $34\ \text{min}$), delaying carbon starvation to **$\tau_{\text{starve}} = 42.0\ \text{minutes}$** but remaining diffusion-limited ($\Delta C = -45\ \text{ppm}$) with extensive rim condensation fogging.
   - *NASA VEGGIE (ISS cabin bellows, $95\ \text{L}$):* Active blower delivers $28.3\ \text{L/min}$ ($\text{ACH} = 17.8\ \text{h}^{-1}$), maintaining indefinite carbon replenishment but lacking enclosed bio-containment or in-situ chemical fixation.
   - *NASA LEAF GBC1 Pot Assembly ($54.1\ \text{cm}^3$ headspace):* Controlled active pull suction ($Q = 0.50\ \text{L/min}$) delivers an extraordinary **$\text{ACH} = 554.5\ \text{h}^{-1}$** (one complete chamber volume turnover every **$6.5\ \text{seconds}$**; continuous fresh air flux of $0.34\ \text{mmol/s}$). Carbon depletion is **indefinite** ($\tau_{\text{starve}} = \infty$), maintaining steady-state foliar $\text{CO}_2$ drawdown within **$\Delta C = 1.86\ \text{ppm}$** ($> 99.5\%$ of ambient concentration) across all 164 seedlings.
3. **Integrated Flight Multi-Physics Advantage:**
   GBC1 combines the bio-containment and compact payload density of sealed Petri dishes ($64.6\ \text{cm}^3$) with the indefinite autotrophic carbon support of open growth chambers ($554.5\ \text{ACH}$), while adding sub-second autonomous chemical fixation ($1.20\ \text{s}$ RNAlater root perfusion) and negative-pressure condensation defense ($< 62\%\ \text{RH}$ lid margin).
